*** Summary ***

The paper presents a hierarchical planning and control framework for
legged locomotion on uneven terrain, extending the a previously
existing work [24]. The system combines: (1) offline synthesis using
LTL-based reactive synthesis with MICP to generate a repertoire of
gaits and a symbolic strategy automaton, (2) online MICP solving for
feet placement selection and feasibility verification, and (3)
nonlinear MPC for trajectory tracking with centroidal dynamics.

The key contributions beyond [24] are: (1) a coordination mechanism
between the symbolic planner and tracking controller that handles MICP
computation delays through two cases (robot waiting when MICP exceeds
NMPC horizon, or seamless trajectory appending when MICP completes
earlier), (2) online pose retargeting to ensure feasibility under
actual terrain, and (3) comprehensive hardware validation with
systematic benchmarking against both heuristic-based and pure MIP
approaches.

*** Assessment ***

Strengths:
- The integration of reactive synthesis with MICP-based planning
addresses a relevant problem in terrain-adaptive locomotion.
- Hardware validation demonstrates practical deployment capability,
which is non-trivial.
- The framework successfully handles runtime uncertainties through
symbolic replanning.

Concerns:

1) Contribution clarity and significance:
The incremental contributions relative to [24] requires stronger
justification. Hardware deployment and delay handling appear to be
practical achievements with limited scientific contribution. The
proposed coordination mechanism (waiting for MICP or appending
trajectories) are straightforward implementation choices do not appear
to be innovative. The paper will benefit from a clearer identification
and articulation of technical and scientific challenges that were
overcome during hardware deployment.

2) Technical clarity and reproducibility.

 - Symbolic synthesis step: How are LTL specifications ($\phi$)
constructed from MICP solutions? Are preconditions and postconditions
manually encoded or learned? Section VI-B states skills execute "when
at least one of its preconditions is satisfied" - please clarify
whether this refers to one precondition set (&#963;_robot,
&#963;_terrain pair) from &#931;^pre_o, or individual propositions
within a precondition. 
 - Benchmark definition: The "pure MIP planner" (Sec. IX-E) needs
precise definition. Does it use the same convex relaxations as MICP but
without symbolic-level guidance? Or does it formulate a non-convex MIP
without relaxations?

3) Calibrating claims with achieved results:
Several claims appear overstated given the experimental evidence:
- "Dynamically changing environments": All experiments use static,
pre-mapped terrains. While runtime repair handles unexpected obstacles
(Fig. 14), this differs from continuous environmental changes.
- "Dynamic locomotion": The hardware experiments primarily use
conservative gaits (3s trotting, 2.25s static walking). 
- "Safety guarantees": The convex relaxations of dynamics (simplified
angular dynamics in Eq. 3, fixed Jacobian in Eq. 14) and lack of
verification between MICP and NMPC layers limits formal safety
assurances. 

4) Limited reactivity and Symbolic-Continuous interaction:
The symbolic-MICP level interaction operates at a low frequency (once
per MICP solve, ~0.37-9.13s per Table III-IV), with no symbolic/MICP
level involvement
during NMPC tracking. The backward communication from MICP to symbolic
level also only conveys infeasibility, and no other environment or
robot-related information that can enhance symbolic planning. This
resembles a classical sense-plan-act cycle more than reactive
synthesis.
Questions to address:
- How does this differ from a single MICP controller that can try out
several gait
sequencing heuristics with a gait-free fallback?
- Could you demonstrate scenarios where symbolic-level reasoning
provides qualitative advantages over purely optimization-based
approaches?
- What prevents more frequent symbolic-continuous interaction during
execution?

5) Baseline Comparisons

The experimental validation would be strengthened by:
-  Improving the heuristic baseline: The baseline (Section X-D)
selecting "nearest rebar polygon and closest feasible foothold" is
overly simplistic. Consider comparing against recent perceptive
locomotion methods. Crucially, fixed gait-timing might also be
unreasonably restrictive being the cause for its failure rather than
any fundamental approach differences. Can you consider more
sophisticated baselines such as, " "Winkler, A. W., Bellicoso, C. D.,
Hutter, M., & Buchli, J. (2018). Gait and trajectory optimization for
legged systems through phase-based end-effector parameterization. IEEE
Robotics and Automation Letters, 3(3), 1560-1567." that are closer to
the state-of-the-art?
-   Pure MIP baseline: This approach requires major clarifications due
to its currently ambiguous presentation.
-   Ablation studies: Analyze contributions of (1) online retargeting,
(2) delay coordination, (3) symbolic vs. direct MICP replanning.

*** Minor Comments ***

Please consider reorganizing sections better. For example, the section.
IV is just seven lines long. 

- While focusing on planning/control is reasonable, the lack of online
perception limits validation of safety claims. Consider discussing how
terrain segmentation uncertainty or perception delays would affect the
framework.

- The rationale for making discrete decisions at both symbolic-level
and MICP-level could be clarified. What does the symbolic level provide
that a better-designed MICP level couldn't achieve? Or alternately, why
can't your symbolic level decide which polygon to step on?
